## Key Statistical Concepts Applied

**Autocorrelation Function (ACF):**  
$$\hat{\rho}_k = \frac{\sum_{t=k+1}^{n}(r_t - \bar{r})(r_{t-k} - \bar{r})}{\sum_{t=1}^{n}(r_t - \bar{r})^2}$$

**Ljung-Box Q-statistic:**  
$$Q(m) = n(n+2)\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\hat{\rho}_k^2}{n-k} \sim \chi^2(m) \text{ under } H_0$$

**ARCH LM Test:** Regress $\hat{\epsilon}_t^2$ on $q$ lags; $LM = nR^2 \sim \chi^2(q)$ under $H_0$ of no ARCH.

## Trading System Implications

**1. Vol-adjusted position sizing is essential.**  
Because variance is time-varying and clusters into regimes, a fixed position size results in wildly different risk levels across market conditions. A position that risks 0.5% of equity in a quiet market may risk 2-3% in a volatility surge.

**2. GARCH-based vol forecasting will outperform realised vol lookbacks.**  
The persistence of volatility (GARCH β ≈ 0.89–0.92) means yesterday's volatility is highly informative about tomorrow's. A GARCH(1,1) model should significantly improve position sizing over a simple 21-day rolling window.

**3. Raw returns are not predictable, but risk is.**  
This is the core insight: while return direction is essentially unpredictable at daily frequency, the magnitude (volatility) is forecastable. Our edge is not in predicting direction — it's in sizing positions appropriately to the prevailing volatility regime.

**4. Fat tails require conservative tail risk assumptions.**  
Normal VaR will underestimate true risk by a meaningful margin. Position sizing should incorporate Student-t or empirical quantile approaches, not Gaussian assumptions.
